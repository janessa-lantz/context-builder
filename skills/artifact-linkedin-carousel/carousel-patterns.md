# Carousel Patterns

Reference for [artifact-linkedin-carousel](SKILL.md). Specs, hook formulas, density rules,
ending patterns, and post-copy conventions.

## Specs

- Format: PDF upload (LinkedIn document post)
- Dimensions: 1080 x 1350 (4:5 vertical) performs best; 1080 x 1080 acceptable
- Slide count: 10 is the sweet spot, 8 to 12 the range; engagement drops past 12
- Static only; no animation or interaction
- File size under 100MB

## Hook formulas

Slide 1 must stop the scroll. Fourteen patterns that consistently work on B2B LinkedIn:

1. Controversial claim ("Most [role]s are measuring the wrong thing.")
2. Counterintuitive stat (a number that contradicts the assumed picture)
3. "I was wrong about X"
4. "Stop doing X. Here's what to do instead."
5. The problem nobody wants to talk about
6. "What [role] doesn't tell you"
7. Transformation story (from [before-state] to [after-state])
8. Framework reveal ("The N-step [process] every [role] should run")
9. "If you're [specific role], read this"
10. Public mistake admission
11. "The truth about X"
12. "N lessons from N years doing X"
13. Prediction with a date
14. Definition-based reframe ("Most people define X wrong")

Ground the chosen hook in the canon: a `point-of-view` carousel argues the company's thesis,
a stat hook needs a real number from `key-metrics` or cited research, a customer-framing
hook uses the language captured in `jobs-to-be-done`.

## Density rules

One idea per slide. Consumed on mobile at 2 to 3 seconds per slide; dense slides get
skipped.

- Headline: 10 words or fewer
- Body: 35 words or fewer
- Total text on a slide: 45 words or fewer

If a slide is cramped: split it, cut half the words, or replace text with a visual element.

## Ending patterns

- **CTA**: drives to a concrete offer. Only when a real offer exists; never fake one.
- **Recap**: a scannable list of the points made. For educational carousels; makes them save-worthy.
- **Checklist**: a practical list the reader will save. For tactical, how-to carousels.
- **Question**: invites comment debate. For thought-leadership carousels.

## Post copy conventions

The accompanying post does three jobs: stop scroll, set expectation, invite interaction.

Structure: hook line (matches slide 1, verbatim or close), one or two sentences on why now,
a preview of what the carousel covers, and a CTA ("swipe through," "save this," "share with
your team").

- LinkedIn truncates around 150 characters; front-load the hook
- 100 to 250 words total; the carousel is the content, the post is the door
- 3 to 5 topical hashtags at the bottom; never generic ones, never front-loaded

## Anti-patterns

- Warm-up slides before the thesis
- Every slide using the same layout
- All text, no visuals (at least 3 slides should be visual-led)
- Text below 10pt at full resolution
- Inconsistent palette, type, or icon style across slides
- A weak CTA slide where a strong recap would do more
